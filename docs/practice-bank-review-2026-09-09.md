## Most consequential findings

1. **More than one valid completion.** Everyday Phrases Q4 offers both Happy and Sorry for “I am ___”. Practice N–Z Q7 offers X and Y for “bo_” (box/boy). Vowel Sounds Q10 offers a and u for “C_t” (cat/cut). Writing Simple Words Q6 similarly allows dog/dug. A previously taught example does not make an unqualified alternative spelling wrong.
2. **Incorrect fraction definitions.** Half Q1 offers “two equal parts” as the intended definition; Quarter Q1 says a quarter means four equal parts. A half or quarter is **one** of the respective equal parts. The quarter source explicitly repeats the error; the half source contains both the correct body definition and incorrect headings/summary.
3. **English targets translated away.** In the actual Arabic learner view, Prepositions Q1 asks the child to complete “The ball is ___ the box”, but supplies على / في / تحت / بجانب. English mode correctly supplies on / in / under / next to. Arabic instructional support should not replace the English word being assessed.
4. **English prompts reveal the requested English answer.** Examples include Family Words (“The English word for mother is”), Colours (“Which English colour word means red?”), and Describing People (“Which word means big?”). These are translation tasks in Arabic, but become copying/matching tasks in English. Use the Arabic meaning as the cue in either UI language, or provide an actual visual/situation.
5. **Source mistakes pass through alignment checks.** The fractions definitions and confusion between letter names and sounds originate in the lesson text. A reviewer that only asks whether an item agrees with its source can approve incorrect teaching. Oxford University Press explains why a letter's name and its sound must be distinguished in phonics. [Oxford Owl: Letters and Sounds](https://home.oxfordowl.co.uk/reading/what-is-letters-and-sounds/).
6. **Missing context introduced during adaptation.** The division source explains 10 objects grouped in twos; final Q10 omits that grouping context and says the quotient 5 is “the number of groups”. The same expression can also describe sharing among two groups, yielding five objects per group. The vowel source's C_t activity refers to a picture; the final Practice supplies no equivalent distinguishing cue.
7. **Weak assessment coverage.** Writing lessons have almost entirely selection/true-false questions, not actual writing. Phonics Practices often permit matching printed letters without identifying a sound. These can support learning, but their scores alone do not establish the named productive/listening skill.

For recurring English translation failures, a consistent repair is: **“Which English word means «الأم»?”**, **“Which English colour word means «أحمر»?”**, **“Which sentence means «أنا سعيد»?”**. Keeping an Arabic semantic cue inside an English-language instruction is appropriate for these bilingual English-learning tasks. Do not simultaneously print the Arabic answer meaning beside every English choice in a scored recognition item; offer translation help as a hint or after the attempt if desired.
